What the numbers mean

The idea, the formula, then your own number.

Volatility

How much the value swings in a typical year. About two years in three stay within one volatility of the average.

σ = std(daily returns) × √252

19.1%: on €31 000, a typical year moves the value by about €5 932 either way.

Beta

How strongly the portfolio follows the S&P 500, both measured in euros. 1.0 moves with the market.

β = cov(portfolio, market) ÷ var(market)

1.02: a 10% market fall has come with about 10.2% for you.

Correlation

Whether a stock moves with the rest of your holdings: 1 together, 0 unrelated, negative in opposite directions.

ρ = cov(stock, rest) ÷ (σ_stock σ_rest)

Nvidia 0.54, Suomi Indeksi 0.29.

On bad days

The same correlation measured only on your worst fifth of days. Diversification that disappears in a sell-off shows up here.

ρ on days where portfolio return ≤ its 20th percentile

Alphabet rises from 0.32 to 0.41: it falls with the rest more often than it seems.

Risk share

How much of the portfolio’s swing each holding causes. The shares add up to 100%.

RCᵢ = wᵢ (Σw)ᵢ ÷ σ²

Nvidia is 13% of the money and 18% of the risk.

Effective holdings

How many equal-sized positions your weights are worth.

N = 1 ÷ Σ wᵢ²

6.4: ten positions behave like about five equal ones.

Worst fall

The deepest drop from a high point during the past year, with today’s weights held fixed.

min (value ÷ running peak − 1)

−16.0% with the current mix.
